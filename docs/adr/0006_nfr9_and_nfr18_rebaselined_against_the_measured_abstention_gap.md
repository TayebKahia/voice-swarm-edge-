# ADR-0006: NFR-9 and NFR-18 are Re-Baselined Against the Measured Figure, and Reported as One Limitation

**Status:** Accepted (Session 05, Sun 20 Sep)
**Supersedes:** nothing. **Amended by:** nothing.

## Context

Surface B (Sat 19) measured two requirements as missed by every one of the six
artefacts, on every split:

- **NFR-9**, safe-failure rate, budget $\geq$ 0.70 --- measured 0.0000 to 0.1579,
  pooled 0.0530.
- **NFR-18**, false-command rate on `test_ood`, budget $\leq$ 0.05 --- measured
  0.1867 to 0.3933.

`prd.md` §10.2 fixes the response in advance: a constraint no configuration satisfies
causes the rule to fail loudly, the constraint is re-baselined against the measured
figure, and the re-baselining is reported --- rather than the rule being quietly
changed. This ADR is that report. The analysis itself is
`results/limitation_abstention.md`, generated by `python eval/abstention.py`.

---

## Decision 1 --- The two requirements are one limitation, not two

They were initially framed as two independent budget misses. They are not independent:
both are the absence of a single mechanism. The grammar guarantees the decoder emits a
**well-formed** command; nothing makes the model decline to emit one at all. Training on
a corpus of valid commands taught it that a command is always the answer --- the same
training that produced the structural reliability Table 19 reports.

Reporting them separately would invite two separate mitigations for one cause. They are
therefore written once, in `results/limitation_abstention.md`, which Master Ch 5 (failure
modes) and Ingénieur Ch 6 (limitations) both cite rather than re-derive.

## Decision 2 --- A safe failure is split by origin, because the rate alone misleads

`safe_failure_rate` credits any error whose dispatched action is `unknown` or `HOVER`.
Reading it as evidence of abstention is wrong, and the per-item files show why. Of the
28 safe failures in 3,540 items:

| Origin | Count |
| :--- | ---: |
| The model emitted `hover` --- a command to hold, which is safe but not a refusal | 24 |
| The validator fell back to `HOVER` --- layer 2 rejecting a provably inconsistent command | 3 |
| The model emitted `unknown` --- the model actually declining | 1 |

The metric cannot make this split by construction: it is defined over the *dispatched*
action, and a `hover` looks identical whichever produced it. So the entry reports the
origin breakdown beside the rate, and `eval/abstention.py:_origin` is the one place the
distinction is implemented.

**This was got wrong first.** The initial implementation classified every `hover` as a
validator fallback, which would have reported 27 validator catches and 1 abstention --- a
system that checks itself --- instead of 24 confident commands, 3 catches and 1
abstention. The claim survives either way, but the mechanism it names does not, and the
wrong version credits `schema/validate.py` with work it never did. `eval/test_abstention.py`
exists to keep that specific error from returning.

## Decision 3 --- The diagnosis is topic-triggered abstention, not absent abstention

The honest reading of the two splits together is finer than "the model cannot say
`unknown`". It can, and does: on `test_ood`, where every reference is `unknown`, the
models abstain 60.7% to 81.3% of the time. The capability is present and reachable under
the grammar.

It is in-domain input where it vanishes: over `test_synth` and `test_golden`, 282 errors,
and the model volunteered `unknown` on an item whose reference was a real command exactly
**once in 2,640**. That one instance was itself an error --- it declined a valid `hover`.

So abstention here behaves as a **topic classifier, not a confidence estimate**. The
system can tell that an utterance is not about drones; it cannot tell that an utterance
*is* about drones and was not understood. This is recorded as the diagnosis of record
because it selects the future work: a confidence gate addresses it, more out-of-domain
training data does not.

## Decision 4 --- The re-baselined values, and what they are not

- **NFR-9: $\geq$ 0.70 re-baselined to the measured pooled 0.0530** (range
  0.0000--0.1579).
- **NFR-18: $\leq$ 0.05 re-baselined to the measured 0.1867--0.3933**, per configuration.

These are **descriptive baselines** --- a record of where the system stands. They are not
targets lowered until they could be met, and neither requirement is reported as met
anywhere in either thesis. The original budgets remain the targets; the candidate fixes
are named as future work in the entry's §7 and are not implemented, measured, or claimed.

Two limits are attached to the re-baselining wherever it is cited:

1. **NFR-9 is sourced to Exp-1 *and* Exp-3** (`prd.md` Table 12), and Exp-3 has not run.
   This baseline rests on Exp-1 evidence only and must be revisited when the end-to-end
   golden-set run lands --- Exp-3 puts real ASR error into the input, which can only move
   the figure.
2. The figures are the deployed surface only (quantised, grammar on). The grammar-off
   ablation surface posts a different and slightly higher safe-failure rate and is not
   quoted, because it is not what ships.

## Decision 5 --- The in-domain error taxonomy is reported beside the rate

`safe` versus `unsafe` is a binary that hides what the swarm would have done. Pooled over
the six artefacts on the in-domain splits, of 282 errors: 240 (85.1%) dispatch the right
intent with wrong parameters, 39 (13.8%) dispatch a different actionable intent, and 3
(1.1%) resolve to a safe action. **98.9% of in-domain errors dispatch an executable
command.**

The largest single family is the **yaw sign flip** --- 114 of 282 errors (40.4%) turn the
swarm through the correct angle in the wrong direction. It is worth naming separately
because it is the failure the three validation layers are structurally unable to catch:
the command is schema-valid, inside the envelope, and internally consistent. No
consistency check distinguishes a clockwise from an anticlockwise intent. This is the
concrete case for why a limitation on *abstention* is a safety limitation and not an
accuracy one.
