\chapter{Results}
\label{chap:results}

This chapter reports what the protocol of Chapter~\ref{chap:method} measured. It is organised
around the experiments rather than around the research question, and the measurements the answer
to RQ1 draws on culminate in Section~\ref{sec:selection}, where the selection rule stated in
Section~\ref{sec:definitions-of-record} is applied to them.

Two properties of the protocol shape every section that follows. First, the surfaces are kept
apart. Accuracy is scored on the workstation: for the selected configuration, the deployed surface
produced identical outputs on the workstation and on the Raspberry~Pi~5 for all 200 golden
transcripts (Section~\ref{sec:definitions-of-record}). Every latency, throughput and memory figure
is measured on the Raspberry~Pi~5 itself. Second, the multi-model benchmark reads the
\emph{transcript} of each test item, not its audio. Every accuracy figure in
Sections~\ref{sec:benchmark} to~\ref{sec:statistics} is therefore measured on reference text, and
is expected to overstate what the deployed pipeline achieves from audio.
Section~\ref{sec:speaker-sensitivity} measures the recognition error that the audio input carries.

Every table in this chapter is rendered by \texttt{eval/tables.py} directly from the measurement
files named in its caption, and no table cell was transcribed by hand. The figures quoted in the
prose are taken from those tables and files, and each was checked back against its source.

\section{Speaker sensitivity at the acoustic front end}
\label{sec:speaker-sensitivity}

The acoustic front end is a source of error that the multi-model benchmark cannot see, and the
speaker-sensitivity experiment measures its \gls{wer} before any language model is involved.
Table~\ref{tab:asr-sensitivity} reports \gls{wer} for \texttt{whisper.cpp tiny.en}~\cite{whisper,whispercpp}
on 300 validated Common Voice English clips~\cite{commonvoice}, bucketed by the accent metadata the
corpus carries, alongside the author's 200 recorded drone commands.

\input{generated/table16_asr_speaker_sensitivity}

Two cautions bound what Table~\ref{tab:asr-sensitivity} supports. The comparison is indicative
rather than matched: the author read drone commands and the Common Voice speakers read general
English, so the two figures are computed over different text, and no pass or fail is claimed from
their ordering. Seven of the fifteen buckets carry fewer than 10 utterances, and their intervals are
reported as \texttt{n too small} rather than as numbers that would invite comparison they cannot
support.

Within those limits, the author's \gls{wer} is 23.1\% with a 95\% confidence interval of [20.3,
25.9], which places the author below 7 of the 15 accent buckets --- the 53rd percentile of the
bucket distribution. The speaker-sensitivity requirement (Table~\ref{tab:requirements}) asks for
that position rather than for a threshold, and it places the author near the median of the bucket
distribution. The spread across buckets is wider. New~Zealand English is transcribed at 6.2\%
[0.0, 16.3] and Hong Kong English at 49.5\% [35.0, 63.6], intervals that do not overlap, and the
largest bucket in the sample --- India and South Asia, 80 utterances --- sits at 27.9\% [22.5,
33.6]. Across this speaker population, the recogniser's error rate varies by a factor of about
eight between buckets.

The deployed configuration passes a domain prompt to the decoder; Table~\ref{tab:asr-sensitivity}
reports the unprompted pass, because only that condition puts both speakers under the same
treatment. The prompt's measured effect is small and its sign is not consistent: on Common Voice it
moves \gls{wer} from 22.4\% to 22.9\% (+0.5 percentage points, pp), and on the author's commands from
23.1\% to 23.0\% ($-$0.1~pp). The prompt is retained because it is what the runtime ships, not
because this experiment established a benefit.

\paragraph{Reference-text input.} A \gls{wer} in the low twenties, for this speaker under clean
recording, is the input the language model receives from the deployed front end. The multi-model
benchmark does not receive it: it reads the reference transcript. The \gls{em} figures that
follow are therefore measured on reference input, not on the pipeline's input. How much of that
accuracy survives audio input, and the net cost of the audio front end (recognition and
segmentation together), are measured by the acoustic-robustness experiment of the
\emph{M\'emoire d'Ing\'enieur}.

\section{The multi-model benchmark}
\label{sec:benchmark}

Table~\ref{tab:model-comparison} is the central measurement of this document: three fine-tuned
\glspl{slm} at Q4\_K\_M and Q8\_0, decoded under the \gls{gbnf} constraint by
\texttt{llama.cpp}~\cite{llamacpp} and scored on three splits on the workstation, with latency,
throughput and memory measured on the Raspberry~Pi~5 for the Q4\_K\_M configurations.

\input{generated/table17_model_comparison}

\paragraph{Accuracy.} On \texttt{test\_golden} --- the reference transcripts of the author's
recorded commands, and the split the selection rule ranks on --- Qwen2.5-0.5B~\cite{qwen25} reaches
0.935 \gls{em} at Q4\_K\_M, Llama-3.2-1B~\cite{llama32} reaches 0.910, and
SmolLM2-360M~\cite{smollm2} reaches 0.760. On this split the 0.49~B model scores 2.5~pp above the
1.24~B model, a difference Section~\ref{sec:statistics} does not find significant; on
\texttt{test\_synth} and \texttt{test\_ood} the ordering follows parameter count.
Section~\ref{sec:iso-parameter} examines the effect of model family at matched size. On the golden
split, intent macro-F1 is above 0.95 for every configuration and reaches 1.000 for Qwen2.5-0.5B. Of
the 152 golden-split errors across the six configurations, 133 (87.5\%) keep the reference intent,
from 79\% for SmolLM2-360M at Q4\_K\_M to all of them for Qwen2.5-0.5B
(\texttt{results/golden\_error\_intents.md}). The remaining errors are thus mostly parameter
errors inside a correctly identified intent rather than misidentified commands.

\paragraph{Schema validity.} Every one of the eighteen rows reports a schema validity of 1.000.
Under the grammar this is a property of the decoder rather than a measurement of the model, and the
only experiment in which the figure is free to move is the ablation of
Section~\ref{sec:grammar-ablation}. It is reported here because the schema-validity requirement
(Table~\ref{tab:requirements}) asks for it to be reported; it is not a measured property of the
models.

\paragraph{Slot-F1 on the abstention split.} Every \texttt{test\_ood} row carries \texttt{--}
rather than a number in the slot column. That split's 150 references are
\texttt{\{"intent":"unknown"\}} and carry no slots at all, so the metric has no dynamic range on
it: with zero gold slot pairs, precision is zero whenever the model emits any slot and the score
can only take one of two degenerate values. What the models do emit there is reported by the
false-command rate below.

\paragraph{Thermal state.} The multi-model benchmark was run twice on the board. The run of
record, whose protocol Section~\ref{sec:definitions-of-record} describes, was made with an active
cooler fitted. An earlier run without active cooling throttled on all 180 scored trials, with the
clock capped at 1.5~GHz against the 2.4~GHz the \texttt{performance} governor sets. Because the
target configuration includes active cooling, that run is not used for selection. It is reported in
Table~\ref{tab:thermal-headroom}, because the throttling requirement asks for the throttle
proportion of every run to be reported, and because the difference between the two runs is itself a
measurement.

\input{generated/thermal_headroom}

The throttle proportion moves from 100\% of trials, against a 5\% budget, to 0\%. Fitting the
cooler reduced decode p95 by 25.1\% to 41.3\% and raised throughput by 35.2\% to 68.5\%, the
largest change being on the smallest model. The uncooled run was capped at, not held at, 1.5~GHz,
so the clock ratio between the runs is at least 1.6$\times$. SmolLM2-360M's throughput gain of
1.69$\times$ is accounted for by the clock alone only if the throttled clock fell below 1.42~GHz,
and the clock actually held was not logged.

\paragraph{Latency, throughput and memory.} On the cooled run, Qwen2.5-0.5B at Q4\_K\_M decodes at
27.93~tok/s with a combined prefill-plus-decode p95 (95th percentile) of 1{,}072~ms, SmolLM2-360M
at 40.63~tok/s and 894~ms, and Llama-3.2-1B at 14.53~tok/s and 2{,}282~ms. Against the decode
allowance of 1{,}100~ms p95 in Table~\ref{tab:latency-budget}, Qwen2.5-0.5B (1{,}033~ms) and
SmolLM2-360M (785~ms) meet the budget and Llama-3.2-1B (1{,}842~ms) misses it by 67\%. Peak
resident memory of the language-model process is 0.68~GiB, 0.55~GiB and 1.63~GiB respectively, so
no configuration is ruled out by the 2.5~GiB memory ceiling, the nearest approach being 35\% clear.

The prefill allowance of 250~ms p95 is the one language-model stage allowance the selected
configuration misses. Qwen2.5-0.5B posts a prefill p50 of 38.5~ms and a p95 of 328.8~ms; only 8 of
its 60 trials exceed the allowance at all. These are the figures of a cached prefix: the benchmark
times with the server's prompt cache enabled, whereas the deployed parser runs without it
(Section~\ref{sec:definitions-of-record}). The items over the allowance are nested across models:
each of SmolLM2-360M's three is among Qwen2.5-0.5B's eight, and each of those is among
Llama-3.2-1B's. These runs do not identify whether input length or cache behaviour causes the
tail.
Llama-3.2-1B's miss is of a different kind: a p50 of 267.7~ms already exceeds the allowance, and 34
of 60 trials do. Under cached prefill, the combined \gls{slm} stage allowance that both figures roll
up into, 1{,}350~ms, is met by Qwen2.5-0.5B with 278~ms to spare. The deployed parser's uncached
prefill, measured by the latency experiment of the \emph{M\'emoire d'Ing\'enieur}, has a p95 of
751~ms, so this margin describes the cached condition only.

\paragraph{Abstention.} Two requirements are missed here, and they are reported alongside the
accuracy figures. The safe-failure rate has a budget of $\geq$~0.70 and measures between 0.0000 and
0.1579, pooling to 0.0530 over 528 errors. The false-command rate on out-of-domain input
(Table~\ref{tab:false-command}) has a budget of $\leq$~0.05 and measures between 0.1867 and 0.3933.
Both are missed by a wide margin: the safe-failure threshold by roughly a factor of thirteen on the
pooled figure and the false-command threshold by a factor of four to eight, and no configuration
satisfies either. Both reflect one tendency: when a configuration is wrong or out of domain, it
emits a well-formed command rather than \texttt{unknown}.

Both requirements are reported as missed (Table~\ref{tab:requirements}). Neither budget is
adjusted, and neither requirement is reported as met anywhere in this document. The likely cause of
the failure is examined in Section~\ref{sec:failure-modes}.

\section{A parameter-matched family control}
\label{sec:iso-parameter}

On the golden split, Section~\ref{sec:benchmark} found the 0.49~B model scoring above the 1.24~B
model by a margin that is not significant. Model family and model size are confounded among the
three deployed models, since each belongs to a different family. Table~\ref{tab:iso-parameter}
therefore adds a fourth model, H2O-Danube3-500M~\cite{danube3}, chosen for the control it provides
rather than as a deployment candidate. At 514~M parameters (Table~\ref{tab:iso-parameter}) it sits
within 4.0\% of Qwen2.5-0.5B's 494~M, so the pair differs in family while holding size
approximately fixed.

\input{generated/table33_iso_parameter}

At matched size the family difference on \texttt{test\_golden} is 6.5~pp, with Qwen2.5-0.5B at
0.935 against H2O-Danube3-500M at 0.870, on 15 against 2 discordant items (exact McNemar
$p = 0.0023$, an exploratory test; \texttt{results/mcnemar.csv}). The larger model of the pair is
the less accurate on all three splits. The 15.0~pp span from SmolLM2-360M at 0.775 to Llama-3.2-1B
at 0.925 covers the full 3.4$\times$ range of size, but its endpoints also differ in family, and no
family appears at two sizes. These data therefore measure a family difference at about 0.5~B
parameters; they do not express it as a share of a size effect.

One caveat constrains the claim and is carried on the table itself. The training recipe is frozen
at three epochs for every model. Validation \gls{em} was still rising at epoch 3 for every model
except Llama-3.2-1B (unchanged at 0.900), from 0.008 for Qwen2.5-0.5B to 0.038 for
H2O-Danube3-500M, so no model is shown to have converged. The lower-scoring model of each contrast
rose most, SmolLM2-360M by 0.025 and H2O-Danube3-500M by 0.038, so part of both gaps may be
convergence under a fixed budget rather than capability. The claim the table supports is about
performance under an identical three-epoch budget, and it is not a claim about the ceiling any of
these families could reach.

Three further differences separate H2O-Danube3-500M from the other rows, and each is declared
rather than corrected. Its chat template has no system role, so the system prompt is folded into
the user turn; the fold is probed from the template rather than keyed to the model name, and the
three original models keep their system-turn rendering unchanged. It was trained in a separate
fine-tuning session (Section~\ref{sec:lora}), and \gls{gpu} training is not guaranteed to be
bit-reproducible across sessions~\cite{pytorchrepro}. It is reported on the reference surface only.
The chat-template parity check of Section~\ref{sec:quantisation-procedure} passes the three
deployed models on all ten audit prompts, on both the formatted text and the token identifiers, but
fails H2O-Danube3-500M on the identifiers of all ten: the two tokenisers segment the identical word
``drone'' differently. A quantised artefact of it would therefore not be the model that was
trained, and no deployed-surface, latency or ablation figure exists for it.

\paragraph{Zero-shot baseline.} The same four models, prompted zero-shot at fp16 on the reference
surface, without fine-tuning and without the grammar, score an \gls{em} of 0.000 on every split:
not one of the 590 items, for any of the four, matches its reference under the comparator (the
zero-shot rows of \texttt{train/kaggle\_out/surface\_a.csv}). The predictions behind these rows were
not retained, so the misses cannot be broken down here into malformed output and well-formed but
wrong commands. What the rows establish is the baseline the fine-tune is measured from: none of the
accuracy reported in this chapter is available from the pretrained models as prompted.

\section{The quantisation delta}
\label{sec:quantisation-delta}

Table~\ref{tab:quantisation-delta} reports the accuracy cost of the deployment pipeline that the
harness of Contribution~C3 measures: the difference between each fine-tuned fp16 model on the
reference surface and its quantised \gls{gguf} artefacts on the deployed surface, on the golden
split.

\input{generated/table18_quantisation_delta}

The deltas span $-$1.5~pp to $+$1.5~pp. Llama-3.2-1B loses 1.5~pp at Q4\_K\_M and 1.0~pp at Q8\_0;
Qwen2.5-0.5B loses nothing at Q4\_K\_M and 0.5~pp at Q8\_0; SmolLM2-360M loses 1.5~pp at Q4\_K\_M
and \emph{gains} 1.5~pp at Q8\_0. A quantised model scoring above its own fp16 parent indicates the
scale of these numbers: at $n = 200$ a 1.5~pp difference is three items, and since precision,
runtime and decoding change together, the gain is not attributed to any one of them. The paired
counts in the table test the reading that the whole column lies within the sampling variability
of a 200-item test set. No difference exceeds three items net, and none reaches significance in the
exploratory McNemar family of six ($\alpha = 0.0083$), whose smallest $p$ is 0.25.

What this delta does \emph{not} isolate is weight precision, and the table's own caption says so.
Three things change between the two surfaces at once --- the numeric format, the inference runtime,
and the presence of the decoding grammar --- and so do the decoding settings and, for Llama-3.2-1B,
the checkpoint (Section~\ref{sec:quantisation-procedure}). The difference therefore measures the
deployment pipeline as a whole. The literature measuring quantisation degradation on downstream
tasks at larger scales~\cite{kurtic2025,kurt2026} reports the isolated effect; the number here is a
different and coarser one, and the two should not be read against each other. One of the confounded
terms is separable with the data already collected. Decoding the quantised artefact a second time
with the grammar disabled holds precision and runtime fixed, and that is the ablation the next
section reports.

\section{The grammar ablation}
\label{sec:grammar-ablation}

Table~\ref{tab:grammar-ablation} removes the \gls{gbnf} constraint and changes nothing else: same
six artefacts, same 590 items, same greedy decode.

\input{generated/table19_grammar_ablation}

Pooled over 3{,}540 decodes, schema validity falls from 1.0000 to 0.9997 and exactly one output is
malformed. \Gls{em} moves from 0.8508 to 0.8492, a difference of 0.17~pp, or six decodes in
3{,}540. On this test set, under these models, the grammar's measurable contribution to output
validity is one decode in 3{,}540.

The number bounds a measurable contribution on these three test splits and nothing more. The
property a grammar provides is a statement about reachability --- no token sequence violating the
schema is reachable under the constraint, for any input --- and a rate on a finite test set does
not measure a property of that form. What the ablation measures is how often the fine-tuned models
depart from the format when nothing prevents them: once in 3{,}540 decodes.

The literature reports that grammar-constrained decoding can distort the model's distribution over
grammatical outputs~\cite{park2024}, and that format restrictions, most strongly \gls{json}-mode
constrained decoding, degrade performance on multi-step reasoning tasks while matching or improving
it on classification tasks~\cite{tam2024}. Neither cost appears in \gls{em} here: it differs by
0.17~pp, in the direction that favours the grammar, on a single-turn schema with ten intents and a
bounded slot vocabulary, a task closer to the classification side of that finding.
Chapter~\ref{chap:discussion} takes up what these two readings imply, and how far a task this small
may be generalised from.

\section{Statistical analysis}
\label{sec:statistics}

\Gls{em} figures separated by two or three items invite over-reading, and the paired structure
of the data supports a direct test. Every configuration decodes the same items deterministically,
so predictions are paired per item and McNemar's test~\cite{mcnemar1947} applies. The statistical
plan of Section~\ref{sec:definitions-of-record} declares two confirmatory families: three pairwise
model comparisons at Q4\_K\_M on the golden split, and three quantisation comparisons within each
model on the same split. Bonferroni correction~\cite{dunn1961} within each family of three gives
$\alpha = 0.0167$ against a family-wise $\alpha = 0.05$. Below 25 discordant pairs, the threshold
the plan fixes, the exact binomial test is used, and from 25 upwards the continuity-corrected
chi-square; both p-values are carried in \texttt{results/mcnemar.csv}, and the verdict agrees under
either test for every row. Two exploratory comparisons were added after the results existed and are
labelled so: the reference-to-deployed pairs of Table~\ref{tab:quantisation-delta}, and the family
control of Table~\ref{tab:iso-parameter}.

\paragraph{Model against model.} Qwen2.5-0.5B and Llama-3.2-1B are not separated: the two disagree
on 5 items out of 200, splitting them 5--0 in Qwen2.5-0.5B's favour, for an exact $p = 0.0625$
against $\alpha = 0.0167$. With five discordant pairs the smallest attainable exact $p$ is 0.0625,
so no split of them could have reached the corrected $\alpha$; separating the two models at this
level would require at least seven disagreements, all in one direction. Both are separated from
SmolLM2-360M at the corrected $\alpha$ --- $p = 2.5 \times 10^{-6}$ against Llama-3.2-1B and
$p = 1.1 \times 10^{-7}$ against Qwen2.5-0.5B, on 38 and 41 discordant pairs respectively, split 34
against 4 and 38 against 3. The measured 2.5~pp gap between the two leading models is therefore not
established by this evidence, while the gaps of 15.0 and 17.5~pp to the smallest model are.

\paragraph{Quantisation against quantisation.} No quantisation comparison reaches significance,
on the confirmatory golden split or on the descriptive ones. On the golden split the Llama-3.2-1B
and Qwen2.5-0.5B pairs each disagree on a single item out of 200 and return $p = 1$; the
SmolLM2-360M pair disagrees on 22 and returns $p = 0.286$. The Q4\_K\_M and Q8\_0 artefacts of each
model are therefore not distinguishable on this evidence; the reference-to-deployed differences of
Table~\ref{tab:quantisation-delta} are tested separately, in that table.

\paragraph{Interpreting the tests.} First, a non-significant McNemar is not a finding of
equivalence. Where the discordant count is 1, the smallest attainable exact p-value is 1.0 --- the
test cannot reject regardless of what is true. What such a row establishes is that the two
artefacts disagreed on one item in 200, a small observed difference, and that is a different claim
from the assertion that no difference exists. Second, identical \gls{em} scores do not imply
identical predictions. SmolLM2-360M scores 0.8125 at both quantisations on \texttt{test\_synth},
and the paired counts establish why that coincidence is not a result: the two artefacts disagree on
24 items and happen to split them 12--12. Comparing summary figures alone would have reported a
difference of exactly zero where there are two dozen; for this reason the paired counts are reported
alongside the p-values.

\section{The selection rule applied}
\label{sec:selection}

The selection rule of Section~\ref{sec:definitions-of-record} deploys the configuration with the
highest \gls{em} among those satisfying both the 2{,}500~ms end-to-end latency budget and the
2.5~GiB memory ceiling, ties breaking toward lower p95 latency. A partial measurement can show that
a configuration misses a constraint but not that it satisfies it, and the rule fails openly when no
configuration satisfies both. Applying it requires the constraint set first and the ranking second. Figure~\ref{fig:pareto} places the three Q4\_K\_M configurations against both
constraints.

\paragraph{Memory constraint.} Peak resident memory of the language-model process is 0.55~GiB to
1.63~GiB, against a 2.5~GiB ceiling that applies to the full stack. No full-stack measurement was
made, so no configuration is shown to satisfy the ceiling; none is shown to miss it either, the
language-model process alone being at least 35\% clear of it.

\paragraph{End-to-end latency constraint.} The end-to-end latency budget bounds latency from end
of speech at 2{,}500~ms p95. The multi-model benchmark measures the \gls{slm} stages only. The
latency experiment of the \emph{M\'emoire d'Ing\'enieur} measured the full parse path for the
selected configuration alone: from end of speech, Qwen2.5-0.5B's p95 is 3{,}122~ms over 226
segments, 622~ms (25\%) above the budget, and the shortest speech-recognition time it recorded is 1{,}063~ms.
Every trial's total is at least its language-model time plus that shortest recognition time.
Llama-3.2-1B's language-model p95 of 2{,}282~ms, measured with the prompt cache enabled, which can
only lower it, therefore implies an end-to-end p95 of at least 3{,}346~ms: it misses the budget on
this evidence alone. SmolLM2-360M, at 894~ms, was not measured end to end, so it is shown neither to
satisfy the budget nor to miss it.

No configuration therefore satisfies both constraints. None is shown to satisfy the memory ceiling,
and the one configuration measured end to end misses the latency budget. The rule's failure clause
applies, and each constraint is re-baselined against the figure measured. The memory ceiling is
applied to the language-model process, the only part of the stack measured, and every
configuration is within it. The end-to-end budget is re-baselined to the measured 3{,}122~ms.
That figure is Qwen2.5-0.5B's own, so the re-baselined budget cannot test Qwen2.5-0.5B, which
satisfies it by construction. It still tests Llama-3.2-1B, whose lower bound of 3{,}346~ms exceeds
it, and SmolLM2-360M, not measured end to end, is shown neither to satisfy it nor to miss it.

\paragraph{Ranking and selection.} Qwen2.5-0.5B at Q4\_K\_M is the only configuration satisfying
both re-baselined constraints, and the rule selects it. Because that outcome follows in part from
Qwen2.5-0.5B being the only configuration measured end to end, it is checked under the reading
most favourable to the others, which admits every configuration not shown to miss a constraint.
That reading adds SmolLM2-360M and leaves the selection unchanged: \gls{em} on the golden split
is 0.935 for Qwen2.5-0.5B and 0.760 for SmolLM2-360M, a difference Section~\ref{sec:statistics}
establishes as significant, with no tie to break. The selection therefore does not depend on
whether SmolLM2-360M would have satisfied the budget. The Q8\_0 artefacts have no
measurement on the board and cannot be checked against either constraint; none exceeds 0.935 on
the golden split, so their exclusion does not change the ranking. The selected configuration is
also the only one of the three meeting the 0.85 exact-match threshold, the 1{,}100~ms decode
allowance, the 1{,}350~ms combined stage allowance under cached prefill, the 20~tok/s throughput
floor and the throttling budget together, and it is not ruled out by the memory ceiling.
SmolLM2-360M is faster on every timing measure and misses the exact-match threshold by 9~pp;
Llama-3.2-1B is the largest model and scores lower than the selected one, by a margin
Section~\ref{sec:statistics} does not establish as significant. Figure~\ref{fig:pareto} plots the
frontier this leaves: Qwen2.5-0.5B and SmolLM2-360M are non-dominated, and Llama-3.2-1B is dominated
on the point estimates. Its accuracy deficit is not significant, so its dominance rests on latency.

\paragraph{Qualifications to the selection.} The margin over SmolLM2-360M is established
statistically; the margin over Llama-3.2-1B is not, and the choice between them rests on the
latency constraint rather than on the accuracy difference, which Section~\ref{sec:statistics} does
not establish as significant. The selection is made against reference-text accuracy, and
Section~\ref{sec:speaker-sensitivity} measures the recognition error that the deployed input will
carry. It is made against re-baselined constraints: the selected configuration misses the
2{,}500~ms end-to-end budget as specified by 622~ms, and its memory is confirmed for the
language-model process only. Like every other configuration measured, it also misses the
safe-failure and false-command thresholds. It is selected as the best available on the criteria
the rule names, not as a configuration that satisfies every requirement placed on the system.
Chapter~\ref{chap:discussion} takes up the consequences of these shortfalls and what would address
them.
