## 1. Verdict

Ready after fixes. The chapter does what prd.md 3.1 item 2 asks: seven foundation sections in
outline order, opened by a statement of purpose and closed by a hand-off, with definitions that
are textbook-correct in every case but one (the gated feed-forward block is described with the
non-linearity in the wrong place). The remaining findings are leaks of Chapter 4 material (a
protocol decision with its justification at line 386, a checkpoint-selection argument that
Chapter 4 makes again, a template-rendering decision at line 84) and two metric definitions whose
wording can be read to differ from tab:metrics although the intended meaning agrees. No BLOCKER.

## 4. UNVERIFIED

1. Finding 6's embedding shares use the published configurations as I recall them (Qwen2.5-0.5B: vocab 151,936, hidden 896, tied embeddings; Llama-3.2-1B: vocab 128,256, hidden 2,048, tied). No config.json is cached in this repository (train/configs/*.yaml name the models only), so the exact percentages could not be recomputed from a local file. The finding stands at any plausible value: even at a 13% share (SmolLM2-360M, vocab 49,152 x 960 = 47 M of 362 M) "nearly all" is loose, and at 21-28% it is wrong.
2. l. 218-219 "no accelerator a language model runtime can use": the Pi 5's VideoCore VII GPU is reachable by llama.cpp's Vulkan backend through Mesa in principle; whether that path is usable in practice on this board was not checked. If the author wants the sentence airtight: "no accelerator that a language-model runtime uses in practice".
3. Finding 11 rests on llama.cpp's importance-matrix option existing in the version cited; I did not open the repository in this review. The citation agent's row 20-22 check can confirm from the same URL.
4. l. 302-303 "the runtime's documentation warns that some rule shapes make sampling slow": the grammars README does carry such a warning in my recollection; left to the citation agent (its row 28).

## 5. Examiner questions (D10b)

1. "You say decode is memory-bound. On a Raspberry Pi 5, whose NEON units are modest and whose four-bit kernels must dequantise every weight they read, is it? What is the board's bandwidth, and how close to the bandwidth bound does your measured throughput come?" The text states the bound conditionally (l. 240-243) and never gives the board's nominal bandwidth or the arithmetic-to-bandwidth ratio, so the condition is asserted rather than shown for this device. Not answered here; it belongs to Chapter 5 or 6 as a one-line roofline check (bytes per token divided by measured seconds per token against the LPDDR4X nominal figure), and the Background could state the nominal bandwidth once the \CHECK on l. 219 is resolved. Finding 7 removes the part of the claim that a jury member would push on.
2. "Masking redistributes probability mass and greedy decoding is then greedy over admissible tokens only. A token-level choice that looks valid can force a low-probability completion later (the token-boundary problem). Does the grammar cost you accuracy?" Answered by deferral: l. 314-318 states the alteration and hands the measurement to Chapter 3 (literature) and, implicitly, the grammar ablation of Chapter 5. Adequate for a Background chapter.
3. "Your decoding is deterministic, so n is the number of test items, 200. McNemar with few discordant pairs has little power, and the test assumes independent items, but your golden items are paraphrases of a dozen command patterns. What does a non-significant result mean here?" Partially answered: l. 397-399 covers power and the non-equivalence reading; the independence assumption is stated only in Chapter 4 (ch3_method.tex:663-665). One sentence in "Paired comparison of two systems", "the test treats the items as independent draws", would let the Background carry the assumption the Method then qualifies.

Level and length. The chapter reads at the right level for a jury member outside the sub-field, with the two exceptions in finding 12 (logits, cross-entropy used before definition). sec:bg-lm (~1,030 words) is the longest section and is justified: it introduces the projection names, tokeniser, chat template and model list that Chapters 4 and 5 lean on. No section is longer than the document needs; the K-quant paragraph (l. 193-210) is dense but every clause is used by Chapter 3's quantisation table or Chapter 4's quantisation procedure.
